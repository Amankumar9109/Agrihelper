# INDIAN CROP CALENDAR, SEASONS & AGRONOMY GUIDELINES

## Indian Cropping Seasons

### 1. Kharif Season (Monsoon / Autumn Crops)
- Sowing Window: June to July (with the onset of South-West monsoon rains).
- Harvesting Window: September to October.
- Climatic Needs: Warm and wet weather during growth, dry weather during grain ripening.
- Major Kharif Crops:
  * Rice / Paddy: Requires high temperatures (25°C–35°C), standing water, and clayey loam soils.
  * Cotton (Kapas): Deep black clay soil (Regur), 21°C–30°C temperature, minimum 180–200 frost-free days.
  * Maize (Corn): Well-drained alluvial or red loams, requires balanced Nitrogen application.
  * Soybean: Excellent nitrogen-fixing legume; optimal in Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Rajasthan.
  * Groundnut (Peanut): Sandy loam soil with good drainage to facilitate peg penetration.
  * Pulses: Arhar (Pigeon pea / Tur), Moong (Green gram), Urad (Black gram).

### 2. Rabi Season (Winter / Spring Crops)
- Sowing Window: October to November (after monsoon retreat).
- Harvesting Window: March to April.
- Climatic Needs: Cool and dry climate during vegetative growth, warm temperatures during maturity and harvesting.
- Major Rabi Crops:
  * Wheat: Optimum sowing temperature is 20°C–22°C. Critical irrigation stages: Crown Root Initiation (CRI) at 21 days after sowing, Tillering, Late Jointing, Flowering, Milk, and Dough stages.
  * Mustard & Rapeseed: Deep well-drained loam soil; sensitive to frost and aphid pest attacks during flowering.
  * Gram / Chickpea (Chana): Prefers sandy loam to clay loam; sensitive to excessive moisture and waterlogging.
  * Barley & Oats: Tolerant to salinity and drought conditions.
  * Potato: Tuberization thrives at night temperatures of 15°C–18°C. Requires friable, loose sandy loam soil.

### 3. Zaid Season (Summer Short Crops)
- Sowing Window: March to April.
- Harvesting Window: May to June.
- Climatic Needs: Warm dry weather with artificial irrigation.
- Major Zaid Crops:
  * Cucurbits: Watermelon, Muskmelon, Cucumber, Bottle Gourd (Lauki), Bitter Gourd (Karela), Pumpkin.
  * Fodder crops: Cowpea, Sorghum, Maize fodder for dairy cattle.
  * Summer Moong and Urad: Fast-maturing short duration pulses (60–65 days) enriching soil nitrogen before Kharif rice.

## Fertilizer Guidelines for Common Indian Crops
- Wheat: Standard NPK recommendation is 120:60:40 kg/ha. Apply half nitrogen + full P and K at basal sowing; apply remaining nitrogen in two equal splits at CRI (1st irrigation) and late tillering (2nd irrigation).
- Paddy (Transplanted Rice): NPK 100:50:50 kg/ha. Zinc sulphate (25 kg/ha) at basal stage to prevent Khaira disease.
- Potato: NPK 150:100:120 kg/ha. High potash requirement for starch synthesis and tuber sizing.
- Tomato: NPK 150:100:100 kg/ha with calcium nitrate foliar spray at fruit set to prevent Blossom End Rot.
- Sugarcane: Heavy feeder; NPK 250:100:125 kg/ha applied across multiple earthing-up stages.
